# How to Contribute

## The Principle

This repository contains a philosophical framework that is open to serious critical engagement. Contributions are welcome from anyone — academic philosophers, scientists, students, autodidacts, or interested citizens — provided they engage with the ideas honestly and substantively.

There are no credential requirements. There are quality requirements.

---

## Three Ways to Participate

### 1. Open an Issue (Ask a Question or Raise an Objection)

Issues are the Socratic questions of this society. They are where dialogue begins.

**Good Issues:**
- "In Section 3, you claim X. But this seems inconsistent with Y because Z. How do you reconcile this?"
- "Has the author considered the work of [Philosopher] on [Topic]? Their argument in [Work] appears to challenge Premise 2."
- "I think the framework could be extended to cover [Domain]. Here is a sketch of how..."
- "The historical case of [Country/Event] appears to contradict the theorem. Can you address this?"

**Not helpful:**
- "This is wrong." (Without explanation of why.)
- "You need a PhD to write about this." (We disagree.)
- "Have you read [Book]?" (Without explaining what specifically in that book is relevant and why.)

Issues should be:
- **Specific** — point to a particular claim, section, or argument.
- **Substantive** — explain your reasoning, not just your conclusion.
- **Respectful** — disagree with ideas, not with people.

### 2. Submit a Pull Request (Propose an Amendment)

Pull Requests are formal proposals to change the canonical text. They are the equivalent of a philosophical argument that, if accepted, modifies the framework itself.

**How to submit a Pull Request:**

1. **Fork** this repository to your own GitHub account.
2. **Create a branch** with a descriptive name (e.g., `extend-theorem-to-federal-systems` or `challenge-premise-3-reverse-causation`).
3. **Make your changes** to the relevant files. You can:
   - Modify existing text (argue for a revision)
   - Add new sections (extend the framework)
   - Add new cases or examples
   - Strengthen or weaken specific claims
   - Fix errors of fact or logic
4. **Write a clear description** in the Pull Request explaining:
   - What you changed
   - Why you changed it
   - What philosophical argument supports the change
   - What consequences the change has for other parts of the framework
5. **Submit** the Pull Request.

**What happens next:**

The community (and the original author) will discuss the Pull Request. This discussion is itself a Socratic dialogue — it is preserved permanently in the Pull Request thread.

If the argument is sound and improves the framework, the Pull Request is merged. The change becomes part of the canonical text. Your contribution is permanently recorded in the commit history.

If the argument is considered but rejected, the Pull Request is closed with an explanation. The rejected argument remains visible to future readers who may find it valuable or wish to revisit it with new evidence.

**Important:** A rejected Pull Request is not a failure. It is a documented philosophical exchange. Some of the most valuable content in this society will be in the closed Pull Requests — arguments that were seriously considered and thoughtfully declined.

### 3. Fork and Build (Create Your Own Version)

If your vision for the framework diverges fundamentally from the canonical version, you are free to fork the repository and develop your own variant. This is the philosophical equivalent of a new school of thought branching from an existing tradition.

Forks are encouraged. They represent the natural branching of philosophical inquiry. The only request is that you acknowledge the original framework in your fork's README.

## What We Value

- **Clarity** over jargon. Say what you mean in the simplest language that preserves the meaning.
- **Honesty** over persuasion. If your argument has weaknesses, name them.
- **Substance** over credentials. We do not care where you studied. We care what you think and why.
- **Precision** over breadth. One well-developed objection is worth more than ten vague gestures.
- **Good faith** always. Assume other contributors are trying to get closer to the truth, even when they disagree with you.

## What We Do Not Tolerate

- Personal attacks on contributors.
- Dismissal based on credentials or lack thereof.
- Bad faith argumentation (deliberately misrepresenting someone's position).
- Spam or self-promotion unrelated to the philosophical content.

---

## Versioning

Each repository's main branch represents the **canonical version** of the framework. The version number follows semantic versioning adapted for philosophy:

- **Major version** (e.g., 2.0.0): A fundamental revision that changes core premises or conclusions.
- **Minor version** (e.g., 1.1.0): A significant addition or modification that extends the framework without changing its foundations.
- **Patch version** (e.g., 1.0.1): A correction of fact, clarification of language, or minor improvement.

The current version is noted in each repository's README.
